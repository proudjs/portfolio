## The problem

### It started at home.

My partner banks with BMO, and everyday tasks kept tripping him up. I wanted to know whether it was him or the app. So over one week in September 2026, with his permission, I ran every transfer and bill-pay flow on his account and wrote down everything that got in the way.

Two transfers were described with twelve different phrases, across screens that didn't link to each other. The full record sat five taps away behind a menu icon.

![Two real transfers, twelve ways to say what happened. Rebuilt in Figma with invented data. Not a screenshot.](<slot:Board of the twelve status phrases> "wide")

## The rules

### Sketches first, rules second.

1. **Five words.** Scheduled, Processing, Completed, Canceled, Failed. Always a word plus an icon, never color alone.
2. **One record, many doors.** Wherever you start, you land on the same record.
3. **One date story.** "Leaves August 11 → Arrives August 14." Always a pair, always in words.
4. **Form, review, confirmation, record.** The same four steps for a transfer and a bill.
5. **Blue has three jobs.** Act, Go and You are here. Nothing else.

## Key decision

### Home tells you what's about to leave.

BMO's Home shows balances and a promo, and nothing about the $350 leaving tomorrow. I kept the accounts where they were and put "Coming up" under them. It shows the next three things to leave, with the day, and each one opens its record.

![Before. Balances and a promo. Rebuilt in Figma with invented data.](<slot:BMO Home> "phone") ![After. Coming up, each row opening its record.](<slot:Coming up> "phone")

## Changed my mind

> #### The blue rule
>
> My first rule was one blue element per screen, a direct reaction to BMO's blue-on-everything. Applied literally, the screens went flat. I loosened it to three jobs. Same discipline, less ideology.
